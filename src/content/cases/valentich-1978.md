## A case that is a document, not a sighting

Valentich is unusual: the central evidence is **a verbatim radio transcript**, not a recollection.
We have, minute by minute, what a pilot said while it was happening — and then we have silence.

That makes the timeline immune to the drift that damages most testimony cases. It also makes the
limits very clear: we know precisely what he reported, and nothing at all about what he was looking at.

## The disorientation hypothesis, stated fairly

The leading investigative hypothesis is that the pilot became disoriented, entered an inverted
attitude, and saw his own aircraft's lights reflected in the water — which would explain lights
"above" him that mirrored his own manoeuvres.

It is a serious explanation and it fits parts of the transcript well. It fits others less well,
including his description of the object's shape and his report of rough running.

## Why it stays unresolved

A missing aircraft and a missing pilot. No wreckage, no radar track of a second object, no body.
The Australian investigation closed with cause unknown, and that remains the honest verdict — this is
an aviation accident of undetermined cause in which the pilot happened to be describing something
unusual at the time.
