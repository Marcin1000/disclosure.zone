## What the file holds

The whole file is three documents from the fifth PURSUE release, four pages in all: the Foreign
Broadcast Information Service’s transcript of a radio report from Rio de Janeiro, with handwritten
notes (EOP-UAP-D001); a telegram from the American embassy in Rio of 14 November 1963
(DOS-UAP-D001); and an airgram from the same embassy of 20 November (DOS-UAP-D002). We searched the
text of all six releases for Bahia, Brazil, Salvador, Conde, Itaparica and November 1963, allowing for
scanning errors; no other document is about this episode.

## The broadcast

The transcript is headed "FBIS 03" and "RIO DE JANEIRO RADIO TUPI IN PORTUGUESE 2330 8 NOV 63 W". Its
whole text:

> "(TEXT) REPORTS FROM THE MUNICIPALITY OF CONDE, IN BAIA, SAY A LARGE METAL SPHERE FELL IN THE CENTER
> OF THE CITY, GOUGING A HOLE FOUR METERS DEEP. THE MYSTERIOUS SPHERE HAS WINDOW SHAPED OPENINGS
> COVERED WITH THICK GLASS. INSIDE IS A HUMAN BODY DRESSED IN HEAVY CLOTHING BEARING INSCRIPTIONS WHICH
> CANNOT BE DECIPHERED. THE POPULATION OF CONDE IS INTRIGUED, AND THE AUTHORITIES HAVE BEEN INFORMED."

It ends "9 NOV 111A RWS/GY". The report names no witness and no official, and it does not say when the
sphere fell.

## Who asked, and who answered

Someone wrote on the transcript. At the top: "SHELDON", struck through; below it "a Curious report.
Please return to me." with initials we read as ECW; then "CSS" and a scribble. In pencil, across the
middle: "asked State (Nesbitt) to let us know if Embassy follows with any info", signed with a name we
cannot read and dated "11/13".

The embassy’s telegram went out the next day. It is addressed for action to the consulate in Salvador
as number 94 and repeated to the Department as 1054, dated "141522Z", "FROM SCIATT SIMONPIETRI" and
signed "GORDON":

> "SINCE RADIOTELEPHONE CONNECTION LAST NIGHT WAS WEAK, PLEASE CONFIRM OR CORRECT MY UNDERSTANDING OF
> YOUR UNQUALIFIED NEGATIVE ON LOCAL NEWSPAPER REPORT TO EFFECT THAT LARGE METAL MANNED BALLOON HAD
> BEEN FOUND VICINITY SALVADOR APPROXIMATELY NOVEMBER 8, DEAD CREWMAN ABOARD WEARING UNIFORM OR
> SPACESUIT WITH STRANGE MARKINGS, THAT FOUR-METER-DEAP CRATER FORMED UPON IMPACT."

> "I UNDERSTAND FURTHER THAT AN OBJECT HAD BEEN SIGHTED ALOFT IN THAT VICINITY AROUND THAT DATE, THAT
> SAID OBJECT HAD BEEN TENTATIVELY IDENTIFIED AS WEATHER-TYPE BALLOON, BUT THAT NO REPEAT NO SUCH
> OBJECT HAD BEEN FOUND AGROUND."

"DEAP" is in the original. The Department’s note on the telegram: "PASSED DEFENSE AND NASA AT 2:20 PM
11/14/63". Its action copy went to SCI, was marked "NOTED" the same day and filed.

The consul in Salvador, Harold Midkiff, replied on 14 November by telegram 157. The release does not
hold it; the embassy quoted it six days later in airgram A-628:

> "Story of strange object descending in region of Conde Bahia apparently fabricated in Rio no such
> objects seen by anyone in the area according to newspaper reporters and others who rushed there
> before investigation reports were received Secretary Public Security speculated object might be
> weather balloon but no such sighting verified."

He also sent by pouch two clippings "from Salvadoran journals", the Diario de Noticias of 9 November
and the Jornal da Bahia of 11 November. The airgram: “The first of these states that the Ministry of
Aeronautics categorically denies that there was any ‘strange object’ in Conde. The other is entitled
‘The Airship that wasn’t there’.” It closes on the Jornal da Bahia reporter, for whom the only
newsworthy aspect of the episode was "an astounding by-product: he sent a telegram to his paper from
the federal telegraphic office in Conde - by an old morse code transmitter - and five minutes later he
had a reply from Salvador."

The airgram was drafted on 20 November by "SA:ACSimonpietri", approved by John Gordon Mein, cleared
with "AA:RDKalb (in substance)" and "POL:CCCarson", and signed "For the Ambassador" by André C.
Simonpietri, Scientific Attaché. The Department stamped it "1963 NOV 24" and, in International
Scientific Affairs, "NOV 26 1963"; a hand added "NOTED - NO ACTION", "11/26/63" and "FILE".

## What is explained, and on what grounds

The status of this case is "explained". What is explained is that nothing fell in Conde, and that the
story, in the consul’s words, was "apparently fabricated in Rio".

It stands on three things:

- **The consul’s inquiry.** He reports what "newspaper reporters and others who rushed there" found:
  no one in the area had seen such objects.
- **The ministry’s denial.** It appears in a newspaper, the Diario de Noticias of 9 November, as the
  airgram summarises it.
- **The embassy’s note.** On 14 November it recorded that "no repeat no such object had been found
  aground".

All three reach us through the embassy’s airgram and telegram, that is, at second hand. The consul’s
telegram 157 and the two clippings are not in the release, so we have his words and the papers only as
the airgram gives them.

What the documents do not explain is where the Rio story came from, and they leave the weather balloon
a speculation: in the consul’s words, "no such sighting verified". The only object in the air in these
documents is the one the embassy understood to have been "sighted aloft in that vicinity around that
date" and "tentatively identified as weather-type balloon". That is why the case is filed under visual
observation.

## A date that does not fit

The subject line of airgram A-628 reads: "Negative Report on UFO (Unidentified Flying Object)
Reportedly Sighted Near Salvador, Bahia on November 8, 1964." Every other date on the same page is
1963: "DATE: November 20, 1963"; the reference, "Embtel 94 to Amconsul Salvador (to SecState 1054) of
November 14, 1963"; the drafting line, "11-20-63"; the stamps "1963 NOV 24" and "NOV 26 1963"; and the
note "11/26/63". Our reading is that 1964 is a typing slip for 1963.

## What the publisher adds

The publisher’s listing behind the release page, uap-data.csv, titles EOP-UAP-D001 "NASC Inquiry into
Bahia, Brazil Incident, November 13, 1963" and says it is "excerpted from the files of Edward C. Welsh,
Executive Secretary of the National Aeronautics and Space Council (NASC)". The page itself names
neither NASC nor Welsh; that attribution comes only from the listing.

The listing’s descriptions of the two cables go wrong in two places. For DOS-UAP-D001 it has the
embassy trying to "verify that the Department had assessed certain content published in a local
newspaper as inauthentic"; the telegram asks the consulate in Salvador to confirm "YOUR UNQUALIFIED
NEGATIVE". For DOS-UAP-D002 it relays "the findings of the scientific attaché at the American Consulate
in Salvador"; the attaché was at the embassy in Rio, which sent the airgram, and the findings are the
consul’s.

## Where it was

The radio report puts the sphere "IN THE CENTER OF THE CITY" of Conde, "IN BAIA"; the consul writes of
the "region of Conde Bahia", and the Jornal da Bahia reporter telegraphed from "the federal telegraphic
office in Conde". The embassy’s telegram of 14 November places the find "VICINITY SALVADOR". The pin is
OpenStreetMap’s point for the town of Conde (relation 20674014), rounded by us to 11.81° S, 37.61° W;
by our calculation it lies about 160 km from OpenStreetMap’s point for Salvador. Our search of the map
also returns São Francisco do Conde, closer to Salvador; that name appears in no document.

## Why this scores where it does

**Witnesses: 0.** No witness is named in any of the three documents. The radio report cites "reports
from the municipality of Conde", and the consul wrote that no such objects were "seen by anyone in the
area".

**Radar and optics: 0.** No instrument recorded anything, and no photograph is mentioned.

**Physical trace: 0.** A sphere, a body and a crater were reported, and none was found: the embassy
recorded that "no repeat no such object had been found aground", and the Ministry of Aeronautics denied
that there was any "strange object" in Conde.

**Multi-channel: 1.** The event itself rests on the Rio media, a radio broadcast and a newspaper report
that the embassy paraphrases. What is independent of them, the consul’s inquiry and the Salvador
papers, speaks against it.

**Documentation: 3.** Three dated documents of November 1963, with telegram numbers, stamps and the
Department’s disposition, show who asked whom and what came back. The consul’s
telegram, the two clippings and the Rio newspaper report are not in the release.

**Anomaly: 0.** No verified observation remains to be measured.

**Data today: 2.** The three documents can be read today. The Rio newspaper report, the clippings and
any Brazilian record are not in the release.

The overall is 0.7: class D, testimony only.

## What would settle it

- The consul’s telegram 157 of 14 November 1963 and the two clippings he sent by pouch.
- The Rio newspaper report that the embassy paraphrased, and the Radio Tupi broadcast itself rather
  than its FBIS summary.
- Any record of the Ministry of Aeronautics, or of the Secretary of Public Security, on the episode.
- Any answer to the request noted on the transcript on 13 November.
