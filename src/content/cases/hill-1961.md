## Why the abduction genre gets one entry, not thirty

There are thousands of abduction reports. They belong to a single evidential category, and one
well-documented example is enough to characterise it.

The Hill case is the right example because it is the origin, because it is unusually well documented,
and because the witnesses were by every account sincere. Nobody in this case appears to have been
lying.

## The problem is the method, not the people

The sighting is one thing: a light on a night drive, reported to the Air Force at the time. That part
is ordinary and was attributed to a planet or an aircraft.

The abduction narrative is a different thing entirely. It emerged **two years later, under hypnosis.**

Hypnotic recall does not retrieve buried memories. Decades of experimental work establish that it
reliably produces vivid, detailed, confidently held accounts of events that did not occur — and that
the subject cannot distinguish these from real memory. A hypnotically produced narrative is not weak
evidence. It is **not evidence about the past at all.**

## What this means for the rest of the genre

Every abduction case that rests on hypnotic recall inherits this problem. That is most of them.

This is not a claim that nothing happened to anyone. It is the observation that the field's dominant
method for producing abduction accounts is one that manufactures accounts — which means the genre
cannot, in its present form, generate evidence regardless of how many reports accumulate.

That is why this database contains one abduction entry and eleven radar cases.
