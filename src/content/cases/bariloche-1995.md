## Why this case is here, and what it is not

Argentina is in this database for a specific reason: the case is a clean example of the commonest
strong shape in the whole field. A crew doing a demanding job at night reports something they cannot
place, several independent parties report the same thing, and then no instrument recorded any of it.

**The multi-channel part is people, not sensors.** That is a real strength and a real ceiling at the
same time.

## What is genuinely strong here

The reports are contemporaneous. The crew reported the light while flying, the tower staff reported
it while working the approach, and the press carried both accounts within two days. Nothing here
depends on someone remembering an evening thirty years later.

The witnesses were also independent in the sense that matters: they were in different places, doing
different jobs, with different views of the sky. A second aircraft holding in the area reported the
same object.

The approach itself is documented, because it had to be. The aircraft was held because Bariloche and
its airport had lost power. That is an operational fact with a paper trail, separate from anybody’s
account of a light.

## What must not be presented as measurement

The reported behaviour, a stop within a few hundred metres of the aircraft and a vertical climb, is
**perceived** behaviour. No radar record has been published. Without one, distance is unknown, and
without distance every statement about speed, size and manoeuvre is a statement about an assumption.
This is the same trap the Sensor Sanity Toolkit exists to demonstrate.

The power failure is the part that gets treated as proof and is not. Two things happening in the same
half hour is a coincidence until someone checks the distribution records and shows they were not.
Nobody has published that check.

## The official position, and why it is unsatisfying

The Air Force conclusion, that there was no evidence of a craft of unknown origin presenting a
concrete risk, is not wrong. It is simply an answer to a different question. Whether something posed
a risk to aviation and what that something was are separate questions, and only the first was
answered.

That gap is the case’s real subject. It is why the file still circulates, and why it resurfaced in
2026 when it was noticed inside a batch of declassified United States material, which transmits the
case without adding a single measurement to it.

## What would settle it

Primary radar recordings from Bariloche approach control for the period, if any were retained; the
full Air Force file rather than its conclusion; and the power distribution log for that night. The
first would convert a sighting into a track. The third would turn a coincidence into a correlation or
retire it.
