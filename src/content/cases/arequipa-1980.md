## Why a non-US military case matters here

Arequipa is one of very few documented instances anywhere of **a military aircraft actually firing on
a UAP**. Whatever it was, an air force treated it as a potential intruder over a sensitive base and
committed ordnance.

That is a fact about state behaviour, and it is why the case belongs in a global corpus that is
otherwise heavily weighted toward the United States.

## The balloon explanation is strong, and worth taking seriously

A large high-altitude balloon accounts for a great deal here: the shape, the apparent hovering, the
inability of a fast jet to engage it effectively, and the eventual climb out of reach as the aircraft
runs out of performance at altitude.

Cannon rounds fired at a balloon at long range would very plausibly do nothing visible.

## What is missing

Everything instrumental. No radar record has been published, no gun camera footage, no unit
documentation. The case rests almost entirely on the pilot’s testimony, given publicly decades later,
with later corroboration from colleagues.

That places it firmly in the interesting-but-unverifiable tier — the same tier as Malmstrom, and for
the same reason.
