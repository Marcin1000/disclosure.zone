## Why this case is in the database

Not for its evidential strength, which is low, but because it shows how far a well-attested **event**
can be from a well-attested **explanation**.

What is documented: eleven people were sufficiently frightened to fire shotguns repeatedly through
the night and to drive to a police station. Officers responded and found gunfire damage. Nobody was
lying about the state of the house.

What is not documented: anything about what they were shooting at.

## The owl hypothesis deserves stating properly

Great horned owls are roughly the described height when perched, have a silvery underside, eyes that
reflect a torch beam brilliantly, and are aggressively territorial at night. Two of them defending a
nest against people with lights fits the account remarkably well.

That hypothesis is not proven either. It is simply the one that requires the fewest new assumptions —
and in a case with no physical evidence, that is the only criterion available.
