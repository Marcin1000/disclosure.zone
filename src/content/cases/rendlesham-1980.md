## The case where the legend outgrew its own document

Rendlesham is the best example in this database of **accounts growing over time.**

Halt's memorandum of 13 January 1981 is a restrained document: it describes lights, ground
indentations and elevated radiation readings. It does not describe a landed craft, touching a hull,
symbols on the skin, or communication with a crew — all of which appeared in accounts given over
subsequent decades.

## What the MOD files say

The British files are unambiguous on one point: **no radar confirmation was found** for an
unidentified object in that area and timeframe, and the case was closed as posing no defence
significance.

That weighs heavily against the case. Rendlesham unfolds between two NATO air bases in a region of
dense radar coverage. The absence of any radar trace across three nights of sightings is itself
significant information.

## What remains real

Elevated radiation readings and ground indentations were documented in real time by an officer.
That is the hard element of the case. Its interpretation — whether the readings were meaningfully
above background, whether the indentations were fresh — remains disputed.
