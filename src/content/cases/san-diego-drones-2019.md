## What the logs record

USS Rafael Peralta’s CIC log opens each day with “USS RAFAEL PERALTA IS CURRENTLY UNDERWAY IN THE
SOCAL OPAREA”. On 15 July (p. 12):

- 20:40 “AWAY SNOOPY TEAM STAR BRIDGE WING”;
- 20:57 “DRONE 1 SPOTTED BEARING 019°T ALTITUDE APPROX”, with the figure lost at the edge of the page, and then
  “BRIDGE VISUALLY SITED [sic] AN EXCESS OF 4 DRONES”;
- 20:58 “DRONE 2 BEARING 044°T” and “DRONE 3 BEARING 090°T”;
- 21:03 “2 DRONES BEARING 253°T MOVING NORTHEAST”, 21:07 “1 DRONE BEARING 249°T”, 21:10 “DRONE 4 BEARING 353°T”.

On 16 July (p. 14): 01:33 “BRIDGE HOLDS 6 DRONES OFF OF STBD BOW” and 01:36 “GUNS VISUALLY HOLDS A UAV BEARING 236°T ON
TIS” (our reading). On the evening of 14 July the log has “AWAY THE SNOOPIE TEAM STBD BRIDGE WING” at 22:10; the entry at
23:00 is blacked out on p. 5, and p. 6, a second scan of the same sheet cut off on the left, leaves the end of that line
legible: “…FLIGHT DECK DRONE.” (our reading).

USS Paul Hamilton’s brief of 17 July, “Interaction with UAS”, charts the ship’s track and the positions of the drones with
a scale bar of 5 nautical miles, marks “Video/Photos capture point(s)”, and shows a small photograph captioned “(U) [UAS] by PHM” with
three dark points on a grey sky (our viewing). Its timeline is blacked out.

The Navy’s incident database has an entry on the same ship, dated 29 July 2019 and placed in the “San Diego OPAREA”: “PHM
observed four UASs with a CPA approximately 200 yds off the bow, port, and starboard beams. The UASs had an on station
time of approximately 2 hours and 20 minutes.” One “hovered approximately 200 yds above PHM”, two shone “what appeared to
be a searchlight or photographic device” on the bow and bridge. “Point of origin of all UASs is unknown. UASs were
observed visually, with optical sighting system, and night vision goggles. Three UASs were identified visually as
quadcopters.” The entry gives local times from 02:40 to 05:30. The Navy’s public-affairs card of July 2020 spells the
abbreviation out: “When an unmanned aerial system (UAS) is NOT immediately identifiable, we refer to it as UAP.”

## What the documents do not say

Who flew the drones and from where: “Point of origin of all UASs is unknown.” No recording or photograph of them can be
opened beyond the small one in the brief. The Navy’s public-affairs card of July 2020 explains why: “Imagery (including
stills/videos obtained by SNOOPIE Teams) of any UAP observation is considered classified”. Rafael Peralta’s log sends its
“SNOOPIE” or “SNOOPY” team to the bridge wing at 22:10 on 14 July and at 20:40 on 15 July, before the entries about
drones. None of these documents names USS
Omaha, under whose name this base held a case until October 2026 (see [the withdrawn entry](/withdrawn#uss-omaha-2019)).

## Recordings of the same year and ocean

Release 05 of the Department of War, on 7 August 2026, has five recordings titled “Unresolved UAP Report, Pacific Ocean,
2019”: [DOW-UAP-PR123](/records/dow-uap-pr123), [DOW-UAP-PR124](/records/dow-uap-pr124), [DOW-UAP-PR125](/records/dow-uap-pr125),
[DOW-UAP-PR126](/records/dow-uap-pr126) and [DOW-UAP-PR127](/records/dow-uap-pr127). By their descriptions the Navy’s UAP Task Force
passed them to AARO in 2022, the first three are films of an infrared sensor display taken with a hand-held device, the
last two films taken by a service member in low light, all five “captured contemporaneously”, and “This media was
digitally altered before being reported to AARO”. The descriptions name no ship, no place and no date beyond 2019.
Nothing ties these recordings to the ships in this case.

## What would settle it

The handwritten logs of other ships in the Navy’s reading room, which we have not read, filed as “2019-07 SPRUANCE”,
“2019- 07 JOHN FINN”, “2019-07 Ralph Johnson”, “2019 - 07 AMERICA”, “USS COMSTOCK_29-31_July_2019” and “SPRUANCE CIC
Logs”. The entries of USS Russell’s deck log from 21:15 to
22:00 on 15 July, blacked out under “(b)(3) 10 U.S.C. § 130”; that evening the log puts the ship at 32°53.2′ N, 119°23.0′
W at 20:00. The photographs and videos marked on Paul Hamilton’s chart and the timeline beside it. The positions and times
of each sighting, and any record of who launched the drones.
