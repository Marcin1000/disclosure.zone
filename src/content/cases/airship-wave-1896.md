## Why a case with no evidence is one of the most important in the database

The 1896 wave has no photographs, no instruments, no investigation and no material. Its evidential
score is minimal. It is nonetheless one of the most instructive entries here.

Thousands of people reported a **cigar-shaped airship with a searchlight and propellers** —
a machine that did not exist. The first powered flight was seven years away.

## What people saw was what the age expected

The 1890s were saturated with anticipation of the dirigible. Newspapers had been promising one for
years. When the wave began, the public had a ready-made template — and reports snapped to it.

Half a century later the same mechanism produced flying saucers after Kenneth Arnold’s metaphor was
misprinted. Half a century after that it produced black triangles. **The shape reported in a wave
tracks the technology the culture is currently anticipating**, not the shape of anything in the sky.

## The lesson to carry forward

Any wave has to be read with this correction applied. The consistency of witness descriptions within
a wave is weak evidence for a real object and strong evidence for a shared expectation. That is not
a dismissal of witnesses — it is a property of perception that applies to everyone, including
trained observers.
