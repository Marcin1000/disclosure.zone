## The most useful solved wave in the database

Hudson Valley is worth more than most unresolved cases, because it is a **controlled experiment
nobody intended to run.**

We know what was in the sky on many of those nights: several light aircraft flying in close
formation with altered lighting. And we know what thousands of people, including police officers,
reported seeing: a single enormous silent craft, often described as blotting out the stars.

## What that tells us

Exactly what the Phoenix Lights argument turns on. When observers see a formation of lights moving
together against a night sky, **a large fraction will perceive and report a single solid object
joining them.** Not because they are careless, but because that is what human vision does with
coherently moving points.

This is the closest thing the field has to a calibration measurement for mass sightings — and it says
the intuition “so many people cannot all be wrong about a solid craft” is simply false.

## The honest remainder

Not every night in the wave had a formation flying. A residual set of reports was never separately
accounted for. That residue is small, poorly documented, and does not carry the wave — but pretending
it does not exist would be the same error in the opposite direction.
